Tolkien

2019

Directed by Dome Karukoski

Synopsis

A life of love, courage & fellowship.

England, early 20th century. The future writer and philologist John Ronald Reuel Tolkien (1892-1973) and three of his schoolmates create a strong bond between them as they share the same passion for literature and art, a true fellowship that strengthens as they grow up, but the outbreak of World War I threatens to shatter it.

Review by wersku

Beautiful yet shallow, emotional yet slightly disjointed. Karukoski hesitates to truly dive into Tolkien's depths, instead he crafts a surface-level beauty that feels more like a reflection than an exploration. But it's not a bad thing i guess but it felt a bit hollow.

The haunting brutality of war, the refreshing winds of friendship, and the sweeping, melodramatic freedom of love. The film paints a visually captivating picture, drawing from its surroundings like an artist seeking inspiration, yet it struggles to ignite a deeper connection. I don't think it feels dull, but rather stumbles along, never quite finding the rhythm or momentum to carry it to its conclusion. It lacks that driving force, that "something" to set it alight.

Review by Georgia Coley 2

Not nearly as bad as I expected, though still not as great as it should be. The direction and execution of the film itself are actually well-near-commendable; the casting (even for small roles) is superb and memorable, and the production design feels rich! It's just the screenplay that feels unmistakably messy and unfocused, jumping from B-plot to B-plot without a clear mission, and then pretending like plot-F or plot-D was the "point of the story" all along. This strange pinball structure makes the film feel like 3 different stories combined into one; and don't get me wrong, all 3 stories are actually pretty compelling! But none of them quite feel like they have time to coalesce with one another properly.…

Review by Joel 6

I’ve been emotionally bamboozled.

At first, I thought Tolkien was going to be a lacklustre slog devoid of any artistic merit - an Oscar-bait biopic that has been told many times before.

I have never been more wrong. Sure, it's a fairly standard story with beats that bumble along a well-trodden path. But it would be wrong if I didn't admit I was moved by the entire thing. That's probably thanks to Thomas Newman's score which enhances the wholesome visuals of love and language against the backdrop of a horrific war.
