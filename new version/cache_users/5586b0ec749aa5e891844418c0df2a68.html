Mississippi Burning

1988

Directed by Alan Parker

Synopsis

1964. When America was at war with itself.

Two FBI agents investigating the murder of civil rights workers during the 60s seek to breach the conspiracy of silence in a small Southern town where segregation divides black and white. The younger agent trained in FBI school runs up against the small town ways of his partner, a former sheriff.

Review by Scout 2

There's a scene where a black man is giving a speech during a funeral. This is one of the only moments of the film in which a black person gets to express his anger. The speech is riveting, but instead of lingering on this man's face, letting the camera draw you in to feel his anger with him, the speech instead plays over mourners walking and the FBI agents shaking hands. In my opinion, this is misplaced direction, aptle only for a film told from the wrong perspective.
