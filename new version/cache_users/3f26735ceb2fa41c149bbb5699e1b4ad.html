Hairspray

1988

Directed by John Waters

Synopsis

The world was in a mess... but their hair was perfect!!

'Pleasantly plump' teenager Tracy Turnblad achieves her dream of becoming a regular on the Corny Collins Dance Show. Now a teen hero, she starts using her fame to speak out for the causes she believes in, most of all integration. In doing so, she earns the wrath of the show's former star, Amber Von Tussle, as well as Amber's manipulative, pro-segregation parents. The rivalry comes to a head as Amber and Tracy vie for the title of Miss Auto Show 1963.

Review by Jacob

John Waters' versatility is wild. Everything about Hairspray is in line with his other work, yet it exists in such a different context. How can you move from NC-17 to PG so smoothly? It's still just as campy and transgressive, but for kids! Waters took what could've been the straightest movie ever made and still made it gay as hell.

It's just so fun. The racial commentary is heavy-handed and smothered in ironic humor, but somehow that all works to make it even more effective. The eccentricity of every character makes even the villains hilarious to watch, the stylized performances and dialogue working wonders. The weirdly cluttered set design just makes everything feel so vibrant. There are some pacing issues, particularly in the dance sequences, but I was thoroughly entertained throughout. Even in a limited role, Mink Stole still steals the show. (There's an easy pun to be made there but I refuse to make it).
