The Limey

1999

Directed by Steven Soderbergh

Synopsis

Tell him I'm coming.

The Limey follows Wilson, a tough English ex-con who travels to Los Angeles to avenge his daughter's death. Upon arrival, Wilson goes to task battling Valentine and an army of L.A.'s toughest criminals, hoping to find clues and piece together what happened. After surviving a near-death beating, getting thrown from a building and being chased down a dangerous mountain road, the Englishman decides to dole out some bodily harm of his own.

Review by Matt! 16

“An extremely volatile and dangerous Englishman goes to Los Angeles to find the man he considers responsible for his daughter's death.” — mmmm not quite.

This movie is virtually me back in college, sitting in a 150-person Chem 101 lecture: its mind nonchalantly drifts about, aware that the plot is moving from scene to scene, yet neither the characters nor the script are really paying attention as to how or why. Inconsequential conversations lead to minor story beats that shift the gears ever so slightly as Terrence Stamp’s primary mission inches onward towards a completion about which we‘ve forgotten to care. At the end, it’s been present enough to earn a passing grade but we walk away feeling as though…

Review by Nakul

Steven Soderbergh's The Limey became an instant favorite as soon as it was over. A solid LA revenge noir that posits battle between two 60's icons - Terence Stamp & Peter Fonda and it's part meditation on age, memory and regret- familial & generational. The movie has some of the most dynamic, inventive, experimental editing that mixes time frames and points of view, it's style adds significant substance to character & plot. Peter Fonda describing the '60s while flossing was my favorite scene. It's ending is wonderfully melancholic as Soderbergh cleverly buses old footage of Stamp from Ken Loach’s Poor Cow.
