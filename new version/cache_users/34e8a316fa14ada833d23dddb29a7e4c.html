Review by Jaime Rebanal 🇵🇸 7

Friedkin pulls no punches here: taking a story about a detective going undercover as a gay man in order to find a serial killer who targets gay men - only for him to find that his own sexual identity isn't exactly what it might have been to begin with, and create a brutally anti-cop movie in that exact process. On that ground, it's easy to see why this would be hated back when it came out - but Friedkin's means of taking all of that to make a thriller where the enemy is one's internalized homophobia only means it's something I'm destined to love.

Only looking forward to rewatching this within the near future.

Review by Will Sloan 2

Okay, it only took half a dozen viewings, but I get it now. Or maybe movies are always just constantly changing? I think watching this right after Querelle helped - another movie where penises and knives are indistinguishable, and gay/straight are not a strict binary - not to mention a few more Fred Halstead movies under my belt.

A lot depends on what you think of the gaze of Pacino's character, and of Friedkin behind the camera. Is it disapproving? Is it frightened? Is it fascinated? Is it turned on? Can it be some combination of all of the above? Isn’t that valid? Fellas, is it gay to go undercover in the gay community and spend all your time standing around looking at gay stuff?
