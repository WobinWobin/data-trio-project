All That Jazz

1979

Directed by Bob Fosse

Synopsis

Prepare yourself for all that acclaim...

Joe Gideon is at the top of the heap, one of the most successful directors and choreographers in musical theater. But he can feel his world slowly collapsing around him - his obsession with work has almost destroyed his personal life, and only his bottles of pills keep him going.

Review by demi adejuyigbe 12

Can't remember the last time a movie made me feel so emotionally drained. I felt like watching a musical today and just brute forced myself into watching this one while the inspiration struck, but now I feel like climbing out of wet sand. Fosse never lets up on the playful tone the film starts with but he slowly mixes in a darkness that makes the levity so grim. It's a shame Leland Palmer didn't act more, because I think her performance here feels so crucial and well-executed. Wish I wasn't so familiar with Bojack Horseman that a lot of the sequences that inspired parts of the show just reminded me of the show, but there's a lot that can't be…

Review by Mike Flanagan 25

Last night, I had the great pleasure of rewatching ALL THAT JAZZ and CABARET back to back at the Egyptian Theatre courtesy of American Cinematheque.

ALL THAT JAZZ is a towering masterpiece, plain and simple, real and true.

I can't think of another film in which an artist so thoroughly and fearlessly dissects themselves in full display of the audience. Riveting, groundbreaking, funny, heartbreaking, profound, tragic, avant garde... Bob Fosse cuts himself open and lays out all the pieces - the good, the bad, and all the rest - in one of the most ferociously original, beautifully rendered films I've ever seen. It contains the best editing I've ever seen in a film, full stop, and its layers of genius…

Review by demi adejuyigbe 9

Cannot really think of another movie like this. We sat for a bit afterwards trying to find the right adjective to describe it since "sad" wasn't really apt, and the closest thing I can come up with is "haunting." The scene of Katie and Michelle dancing is so heartbreaking on a second watch. He could've had it all. I don't think there are a ton of parallels in my life to Bob Fosse's (don't have a daughter, not working on "Chicago," black, etc.) but I couldn't help but see myself in the few parallels that do exist, which made it all the more bleak and made me all the more "wanting to go to the gym." Honestly a great movie to watch the same week you try to quit smoking cigarettes.
