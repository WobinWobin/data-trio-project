Swimfan

2002

Directed by John Polson

Synopsis

His biggest fan just became his worst nightmare.

Ben Cronin has it all: the admiration of his many friends, a terrific girlfriend, and he's on the fast-track to an athletic scholarship. Ben's rock-solid, promising future and romance are turned upside-down with the arrival of Madison Bell. Madison, the new girl in town, quickly sets her sights on the impressionable Ben. While their first few meetings are innocent enough, the obsessive and seductive Madison wants more ... much more.

Review by Lou (rhymes with wow!) 7

This movie feels so early aughts. It also feels like a CW drama transported to the big screen, with a lot of the teens being well into their twenties.

I actually thought Erika Christensen was very solid as the psychotic swimfan. Jesse Bradford's dopey facial expressions (which are 85% of this movie) cracked me up. Shiri Appleby was basically every teenage boy's fantasy: sweet, understanding and sexually active.

Sure it's cheesy, but it's also very enjoyable.

Review by Cinematic Underdogs 5

Swimfan was one of the formative erotic thrillers of my teenage years. As an adult, I still have no issue shamelessly indulging in the liquid pleasures offered by this salty tale of steamy sex & psychopathic blackmail. Erika Christensen glibly oozes every quadrant of the femme fatale archetype with slimy glee — playing part girl boss, part clingy termagant, part sociopath, part homicidal stalker. Her hardened, implacable glare sears into the screen — only to be subtly subverted by a devious smirk. It’s a simple expression but it conveys the film’s menacing message clearly — Horny boys, everywhere, beware!
