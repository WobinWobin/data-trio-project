Review by Sean Fennessey 10

Best first watch of 2021 so far. This had been a gap for a long time and its standing as a breathless, relentless visual orgy easily overwhelmed its reputation as a "shocking" studio production. Derek Jarman was still in his twenties when he created a production design that collided modernist style into a baroque 17th century French setting. Oliver Reed is in his terrifying but in the good way phase, symbiotic with Ken Russell on their fifth feature together. Russell's frantic whip-pans and high-angled framing of omniscient power and punishment irradiate Vanessa Redgrave's mad-eyed performance as Sister Jeanne, the scorned, manipulated tool of the church, to make this hysteria cinema at its most unrestrained. It's fun! (Michael Gothard as the zealot Father Barre is going for it bigger and more fearlessly even than Reed.) It's also a tidy artifact of false panic. Nude nuns, licentious priests, and ritual torture. Imagine that.
