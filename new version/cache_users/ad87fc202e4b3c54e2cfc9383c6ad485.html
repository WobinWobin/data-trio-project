Synopsis

Vigilante, city style — judge, jury, and executioner.

After his wife is murdered by street punks, a pacifistic New York City architect becomes a one-man vigilante squad, prowling the streets for would-be muggers after dark.

Love this sicko movie with its dirty bleak 70’s vibes as well as Winner’s tight (and undervalued) direction, and Bronson’s 20 year dedication to the role of judge, jury, and executioner in a franchise that gets wilder with each entry.

Review by Branson Reese

Wish I could find more to like in this downbeat, acid jazz drenched snapshot of NYC in the mid 70s full of actors whose heads looked carved out of dried up apples. But this really is a conservative piece of art. Look, I'm not opposed to conservative art. It's getting made whether I like it or not and I've loved plenty of reactionary slashers so it's not like I'm up on some high horse, here. I'm no liberal. This movie's real sin is that it's slack and ultimately pretty boring. It's The West Wing without any screwball dialogue. But instead of owning Republicans with facts and logic this is a movie about owning minority teenagers with bullets. Ultimately they boil down to the same message: I'm right and these other people are so wrong that they must be destroyed.

Review by Slig001

Classic revenge movie about a mild mannered architect who becomes a vigilante after his wife is murdered by vicious street thugs. The film works thanks a strong focus on the character. His awakening which sees him realise muggers are bad and guns are a good tool for getting rid of them is calm and measured; and fascinating to watch. The way that the film explores the vigilante theme is interesting too for the way that the revenge isn't focused on the perpetrators of the original crime, but crime in the city in general; which also allows the film to provide a broader commentary on the city itself and local police force. Charles Bronson is spectacular in the lead role and he really lifts the material with a powerhouse performance. This is top quality gritty seventies cinema!
