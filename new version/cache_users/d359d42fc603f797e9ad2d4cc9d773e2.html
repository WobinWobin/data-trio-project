Synopsis

When does a close friend become too close?

Chuck and Buck are childhood best friends whose lives have taken very different paths. While Chuck moved away and now has a real life, Buck stayed behind and developed a dangerous fixation—on Chuck's life.

Review by Dave 1

What starts out as a creepy, spider-tingling obsession flick turns into something of a revelation: a sweet, thoroughly moving drama about a man and his dark shadow.

It was a stroke of genius to cast lesser-known actors (Lupe Ontiveros, Mike White and the Weitz brothers are natural born performers; the screen loves them).

"Chuck & Buck" is a very daring film that so easily could have been appalling and dangerous. But it fell into the right hands and it’s a film that can be universally embraced.

Review by Charlie Nash

As a gay man who's had unrequited crushes on straight men, this pitch-black comedy had me howling and squirming in equal measure. Mike White's protagonist certainly crosses the line in regards to personal boundaries and socially acceptable behavior, but the heartache he feels in regards to the loss of his first romantic/sexual partner stems from a sense of shame that silences (and stunts) all too many queer individuals trapped within our heteronormative society. In other words, this film hit very close to home for me, resulting in a viewing experience that was as upsetting as it was deeply cathartic.
