Silence

Directed by Martin Scorsese

Synopsis

Sometimes silence is the deadliest sound.

In the 17th century, two Portuguese Jesuit priests travel to Japan in an attempt to locate their mentor, who is rumored to have committed apostasy, and to propagate Catholicism.

Review by Karsten 7

I'm....hm....uh....
Writing about movies is hard lol, writing about Silence is hell.

At the ripe age of 22 I still don't know where I stand on religion, I just have a lot of opinions on it that nobody wants to hear about. But what I'm trying to say is I feel like with a film like this, a film as subtly extreme and exhausting as this, your view on religion is going to impact how you take in this movie. Which is why I think the most impressive part about Silence, aside from how it looks, is the objectivity of it, I think. It's taking you in with open arms and dragging you through a whole lot of honesty. Brutal ass honesty. I did a lot more thinking than I did feeling with this one and I'll definitely be coming back to it some day. Yet another classic gangster film from the one and only Martin Scorsese!

Review by George Carmi 34

patient and painful deconstruction of faith and religion. the haunting beauty of seeking meaning in a world that often seems to crumble... constantly rejecting our deepest yearnings. what is faith but a distant flickering light with promise of warmth... yet so fragile that its mere existence can seem like a wicked illusion. perhaps a savior, or a teacher, devoid of answers, leaving us to question our own understanding of belief. simultaneously giving hope while becoming a source of agony when its religious teachings fail. a harsh reality. the profound dichotomy of what it means to be human painted as a challenging theological dilemma questioning suffering, a silent god, and the meaning of putting faith into practice. insane picture. scorsese at his most meticulous (and possibly his best).
