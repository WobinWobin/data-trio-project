Review by Karsten

the ocean’s eleven influence is there but goldhaber citing a man escaped as an inspiration is the more palpable reference. there’s the way it uses its own title to build tension, sure, but i walked away more impressed by the bressonian influence in not having one misstep in the edit and the cinematography being so simple and intentional that it makes it look easy. somehow minimalist and extreme at the same time. just lights a fire under your ass by the end the way la haine and do the right thing do. but for how much influence it wears on its sleeve, the film stands alone as its own angsty take on the contemporary heist film that rightfully captures its urgency 💥💥

Review by i_z 15

This review may contain spoilers. I can handle the truth.

It pains me to be a hater, but if the response at TIFF is any indication, you will be hearing a lot of hype for this movie. Do not trust it. This is the Ocean's 8 of eco-terrorism thrillers: a film whose noble and worthy intentions cloud the shameful laziness of its storytelling.

The first rule of a good heist movie is that things do not go according to plan. In this movie (bad), everything goes off without a hitch. Even the one major complication seeded from the outset is revealed to be a ruse — there was never really any chance that these brave, high-minded radicals would fail. The characters, with one exception, are either non-entities or hacky tropes…
