Millie Lies Low

2021

Directed by Michelle Savill

Synopsis

Fake it till you break it.

A panic attack causes Millie to miss her flight to New York. Everyone thinks she’s made it though, thanks to her fake Instagram posts from the “Big Apple”. A tragicomic hunt for a new ticket ensues, as she attempts to beat the clock, her fears and the truth.

Review by BeBraveMorvern 1

“I’m not interested so much in how people survive as in how they avoid humiliation.”
- playwright John Guare

I kept thinking of that quote as it’s the central driver behind this squirmy, quarter-life-crisis comedy/drama. Millie goes to extraordinary lengths to avoid humiliation. By the end, in a hushed scene that had me clutching my throat, she’s choosing between competing humiliations. 

Michelle Savill’s feature debut touches on imposter syndrome and Indigenous assimilation. But it’s all set off by Millie having a panic attack, and the movie tracks their recurrence and her negotiating her own denial. 

As Millie, Ana Scotney alternates fake smiles with crushing looks of self-recrimination; in a cannily used device, she’s often dwarfed her own image from a PR campaign. Māori character actress Rachel House plays her mom as loyal, thoughtful, and blunt. Their scenes together are among the film’s strongest.

Review by Wes

Absolute crime that this didn't catch on outside of Aotearoa - or inside of it, really. My only reservation is that it's a little cleanly-resolved; there are tensions which get squared away but which could probably have been left out to bang your proverbial shins on. Short of that you've got Scotney (a revelation) doing a stellar riff on nighttime odyssey flicks like After Hours and Good Time - films with New York as a character which Millie exploits as an absence. Maybe the first portrait of Wellington which I've felt presents my city properly?

Review by Kurdt

A New Zealander with an impending architecture scholarship in New York is about to move there, only to have a panic attack and get off the plane. No refunds, and she’s broke. But all her friends and family think she’s in New York? Time to fake it.

Here’s what I really liked about this: it’s partly about how social media puts immense pressure on how we’re perceived by others, and Millie is definitely obsessed with that, but she’s not a completely insufferable influencer-type.
