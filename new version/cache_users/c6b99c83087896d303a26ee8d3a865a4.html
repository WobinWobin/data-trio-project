Oblivion

2013

Directed by Joseph Kosinski

Synopsis

Earth is a memory worth fighting for.

Jack Harper is one of the last few drone repairmen stationed on Earth. Part of a massive operation to extract vital resources after decades of war with a terrifying threat known as the Scavs, Jack’s mission is nearly complete. His existence is brought crashing down when he rescues a beautiful stranger from a downed spacecraft. Her arrival triggers a chain of events that forces him to question everything he knows and puts the fate of humanity in his hands.

He has a partner in a high tower that helps him find these things, and in between missions he finds tchotchkes from “old Earth” times which he brings to an oasis-like area where he plays records and lays in the grass by a lake.

Review by Silent J 6

You ever watch a film that isn't bad, but makes you wish you were watching something similar or better? Oblivion is one of those films.

Oblivion's effects, cinematography, and set pieces look absolutely dazzling and it's the biggest thing the film has going for it. The problem with that is the creators didn't put nearly as much investment in the characters/story as they did these effects. It's easy to get lost in the SFX, but I couldn't care about any of the characters. I already couldn't get into the story without watchable characters, I couldn't get into the film as a whole. The characters are pretty much one-dimensional clichés and the story is as predictable and unoriginal as they come.…

Review by DirkH 17

Oblivion is a science fiction potpourri that borrows so much from so many films it completely forgets to create its own identity in the process. It is a plate filled with lovely ingredients, but it's not a dish. Not even by a long shot.

The conceit and setting, while not very original, are fine, filled with a great deal of potential. And for the better part of the first act, I thought I was finally going to be treated to some original Sci-Fi. But alas, after not too long a time two things happen. Firstly, the plot starts telegraphing where it wants to go so obviously that it becomes nigh impossible to not play out the rest of the film…
