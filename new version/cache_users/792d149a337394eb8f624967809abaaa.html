The Wood

1999

Directed by Rick Famuyiwa

Synopsis

From boyhood to manhood, you can always count on your best friends.

In the panicky, uncertain hours before his wedding, a groom with prenuptial jitters and his two best friends reminisce about growing up together in the middle-class African-American neighborhood of Inglewood, California. Flashing back to the twenty-something trio's childhood exploits, the memories capture the mood and nostalgia of the '80s era.

Review by Silent J 1

I never realized this until now, but I think The Wood might be my favorite coming of age movie.

There aren't many films out there which speak to the black childhood experience. That isn't to say that it's hard for me to relate to a coming of age tale if it isn't told from the point of view of black characters, but for me, the black voice is a distinctly told and unquestionably recognizable one at that. In short, the black experience is a unique one which rarely gets told in bildungsroman movies. That black adolescent experience is told here and it is so refreshing.
