Synopsis

China, 1920. One Master, Four Wives.

In 1920s China, 19-year-old Songlian becomes a concubine of a powerful lord and is forced to compete with his three wives for the privileges gained.

Somber, tantalizing, and contemplative, Raise the Red Lantern is a thought-provoking and powerful portrait of a young woman who, after marrying a significantly older and wealthier man, finds herself confronted with the surreptitious conspiracies pervading the relationships between his three other wives—and soon, her relationships, too.

What begins with a static, yet powerfully stirring closeup of our protagonist, Songlian, soon unfolds into a nearly bewitching picture of four women constantly pitting against each other only to win their husband’s affection for a mere night—and with it, the brightly lit red lanterns raised in front of their house. Review by 🌻 lindsay 🌻 8

This film as a craft is so masterfully made. Everything about it technically is stellar: the cinematography, costumes, sets, score. All impeccable. At times it felt like I was looking at paintings; this film is so beautiful. 

The story is also extremely powerful and one of my favorites that I’ve seen. It’s a story that is relevant at any time and any culture. Women are constantly pitted against each other for the favor of men and it can be so traumatizing and lead to catastrophe. 

This film is quiet with tragedy. Tender with how it depicts these women and their choices. Their oppression is seeped into the story slowly and calmly until it’s become the norm without you realizing it. Easily one of the best, most beautiful, and most sorrowful films I’ve seen.
