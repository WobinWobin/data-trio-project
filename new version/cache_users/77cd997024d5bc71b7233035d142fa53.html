Review by Nakul 10

Watched Peter Jackson's King Kong for the first time in forever. It rules so hard. This movie is extremely underappreciated and doesn’t get talked about as often as it should. An unapologetically melodramatic, romantic, indulgent, and a grand old fashioned adventure story realized with genuine artistic flair. Epic in every sense of the word, with a truly colossal scale to match. Every penny of it's crazy budget is shown on screen through stunning visual effects & cinematography. There are so many scenes where Jackson's horror roots are on full display, stylistically and technically. Also, that T-Rex & Kong fight sequence goes so hard, one of the best action sequences I've ever seen. A lot of people hate-circlejerk over its runtime, but it has excellent world building and characters with actual motivations and development.

They don't make 'em like this no more.

Review by Jake Cole 7

God, I love this movie and I don't care who knows it. What turned out to be Jackson's last gasp as a filmmaker of true vision and excitement, which is all the more impressive considering he takes a limited and already-remade concept and strips it of its underlying racial metaphor to simply delight in the thing itself. But this just bursts with energy, from a well-defined human cast with across-the-board strong performances (even an arguably miscast Jack Black acquits himself well by turning his naturally buoyant energy into something curdled and mean) to its amazing creature designs. To this day I'm wigged out by the bug scene, with its glacial, almost oneiric scoring and the slow pace of the beasts…

Review by Joe A 4

There are moments where King Kong feels larger than life— the intimate moments between Kong and Ann or the bug pit— really showcase Jackson flexing his cinematic muscles. So it’s all the more frustrating to see it fumble through unnecessary detours and become distracted by its own scale. 

In so many ways, it’s a cinematic miracle exists, but I suppose it’s not too strange they gave the guy who did LOTR a blank check to do whatever he wanted, which ultimately feels like a blessing and curse.

Review by SilentDawn 12

70

Peter Jackson's Snyder Cut. Nothing is left on the cutting room floor. You'd think that the 3 hour and 20 minute extended edition would add further characterization and dramatic beats but it's mostly additional scrimmages with Skull Island creatures, as if it didn't already have enough!

Love how nasty this is. Truly a blank check epic. The scope of it is overwhelming but it doesn't hold back. More than half of the movie is PJ and Weta Workshop going crazy with brutal dinosaur encounters and disgusting insects. That final third is brutal and emotionally devastating. Can't say it doesn't have tell-tale signs of where Peter Jackson's filmography was heading, but there's plenty of his early horror/adventure spirit in this movie, and that's enough for me.
