Finding Forrester

2000

Directed by Gus Van Sant

Synopsis

In an ordinary place, he found the one person to make his life extraordinary.

Gus Van Sant tells the story of a young African American man named Jamal who confronts his talents while living on the streets of the Bronx. He accidentally runs into an old writer named Forrester who discovers his passion for writing. With help from his new mentor Jamal receives a scholarship to a private school.

You’re the man now dog!

That line of dialogue has haunted me for 20 years. It’s just such an odd phrase.

I know this movie is a giant list of screenplay cliches, but man I’m a sucker for it…even as my brain screams to reject it, my heart takes over and pulls me in.

I’ve always been fascinated by the mystique of Salinger and to watch Sir Connery riffing on him is simply fantastic.

Why is it the words we write for ourselves are always so much better than the words we write for others?

Review by Filipe Furtado 1

A deeply unresolved marriage between the narrative rote and lovely observed snapshots. Pretty much everything that has nothing to do with plot is wonderful, with Van Sant’s careful gaze and good ear in good form. But when it has to move things along, Finding Forrest turns uncertain, unsure if it should play up the more maudlin elements of its story or try to tone them down. This is still a lot better than its rep as assembly line oscar hopeful suggests, the good stuff is truly goodand Brown and Coonery have a very nice rapport the allow the film to move along as well as possible under circunstances.
