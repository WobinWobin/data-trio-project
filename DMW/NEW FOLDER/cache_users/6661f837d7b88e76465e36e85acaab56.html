Review by King #adoptdontshop 2

Invincible is a short about a troubled teen’s final days. This film came hard hitting and fast, bringing us into the psyche and frustrations of a young man who feels trapped, angry, and lonely. His world seemed like a cage, as Invincible succeeded in surfacing the emotions of young Marc out in the open, accentuated by a heartfelt and emotionally consuming performance by Léokim Beaumier Lépine. Props as well to good direction and beautiful cinematography, and color hues. 

The real life inspiration of Invincible occurred in 2008, so I’m personally grateful that we’ve came a long way in understanding mental health issues, allowing mentors and parents to empathize without judgement.

Review by ZacharyBinx

Maybe the most gorgeously filmed of the shorts I've seen so far and beyond that, it captures adolescence really well. The yearning for freedom and understanding is sometimes so strong that our undeveloped brains make irrational decisions. For me it was sneaking out of my parent's house at night, but sometimes that desire to be free takes a much darker turn like it did with Marc. Rather than focusing on his death, it takes us through the last 2 days of his life and even if we can't know the why, we can see a kid that was battling himself and authority only so he could learn more, be more, and feel less trapped. It's tragic, but the best we can do is try to learn and improve the ways we shape our children's minds.
