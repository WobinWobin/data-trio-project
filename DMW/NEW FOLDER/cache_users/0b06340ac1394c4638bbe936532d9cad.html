Review by Gentry Austin

“You’re ravishing.”
“You’re sick.”

Hard for me to dislike a tongue-in-cheek, Puerto Rico-shot Roger Corman quickie that references Lake Minnetonka. Corman doing a sendup of 50s monster movies, spy capers and film noir in just five days with leftover scraps, dollar-a-day extras and Brillo pads. This is funny! A joyously stupid film that features stolen Cuban gold, a “finky monster,” a former gun moll with multiple pistols in her waistband and an idiot narrator who says things like “it was dusk, I could tell because the sun was going down.” Surprising amount of competent underwater photography. I think the creature looks great.

Review by mike5577 2

Creature from the Haunted Sea is an amusing little B movie directed by Roger Corman in just 5 days. The plot is some American gangsters help some Cubans escape their country with the national treasury. However the gangsters decide to murder the Cubans and keep the money for themselves. To get away with it, they concoct a story that the Cubans were killed by a sea monster. Unbeknownst to the gangsters, a real sea monster is lurking nearby and starts bumping off the cast one by one.
Lots of fun to be had for devotees of cheaply made bad acting movies and fans of Roger Corman.

Review by Klon 3

Myself? I love dopey early 60s comedy and I love raggedy monster movies which means I love Creature from the Haunted Sea. I also love this era of Corman’s output, there’s tons of energy, creativity and confidence. He (or at least all of the people he worked with) knew how absurd monster movies could be so they laughed all the way up to the end when we finally meet the threadbare, googly eyed Creature. This feels like a 1940s East Side Kids ghost comedy set around the Cuban Revolution and written by the Usual Gang of Idiots.

Review by 𝕭laze the 𝓐ction 𝕵unkie

An early Corman spoof of monster and spy movies. For some reason my cut was 74 minutes long, and I can't tell what was added that might not have been on whatever cut is listed here at 63 minutes. The entertainment value here is mostly in the subtle comedy and the goofy look of the monster, for which the deviled-egg-eyed lettuce monster might have 2 or 3 minutes of screen-time. There aren't many laugh-out-loud jokes, but the subtlety of the comedy used is genuine Corman style. The film might not be exciting or interesting, and is admittedly pretty bad, but it is Corman's style of fun.

"It was dusk. I could tell because the sun was going down."
