Although Love Liza had a pretty much, average rating. I did enjoy it more than I thought I would have. Particularly, I liked the cinematography and the attempted but also partly sincere narrative based around heavy bottomless grief.


 Without a doubt, this film wouldn't have been as good without the profound acting by Phillip Seymour Hoffman. He could totally convey emotions in the most moving, powerful, and convincing way possible. It was a touching film to me and deserves some sort of recognition for some of its clear realistic points, even though it did have its patches. You might be into it, you might not be, it depends on what you enjoy, I suppose.

Review by Hunter ᴴᴰ

Grief is a funny thing.

Love Liza takes those hard moments of grief and adds the dark comedy found in real life to create a poignant film about loss. Everyday we use coping mechanisms, but sometimes we use them to isolate or hurt ourselves. Love Liza examines a man so deep in a pit of grief he turns to any escapism, in this case huffing gasoline, to blur the lines of his painful reality and a bliss of nothingness.

Phillip Seymour Hoffman gives an authentic performance that might be my favorite performance of his artistic career. There’s an extra underlying of uncanny truth with his past of addiction and untimely death. There’s depth that it’s not only his wife’s suicide…
