Porcelain War

2024

Directed by Slava Leontyev, Brendan Bellomo

Synopsis

Under roaring fighter jets and missile strikes, Ukrainian artists Slava, Anya, and Andrey choose to stay behind and fight, contending with the soldiers they have become. Defiantly finding beauty amid destruction, they show that although it’s easy to make people afraid, it’s hard to destroy their passion for living.

Review by Kevflix And Chill

Sundance 2024
A trio of artists remain in Ukraine despite advancing Russian forces. They answer their call to take up arms and simultaneously produce art that highlights the beauty of their homeland. Compelled to continue to create because the alternative would almost certainly result in cultural erasure. This would be a compelling companion piece to the Oscar nominated doc 20 Days in Mariupol. Fascinating watch. One minute you’re fighting a war, dropping drone strikes directly into the hatch of a Russian Tank (incredible footage btw), then you drive home where your wife has made you lunch and you paint porcelain sculptures together. It’s hard to truly comprehend what that experience would actually feel like.

Review by Marya E. Gates

If, as Stefanov says early in the film, Russia’s targeting of those who make culture, the writers and artists and teachers and musicians, is how they plan to destroy Ukraine, what happens when those very people are made to give up their work and become soldiers? What effect does that have on them psychologically? What effect does it have on the culture? These questions are left unasked in favor of broad, ponderous statements about good versus evil.

While Sefanov may be too close to the material to ask these hard questions, his co-director Bellomo should have had the required distance to not just compile footage, but contemplate what it all means. To try and find the balance between the violence and the art. As it stands, instead “Porcelain War” itself is its own unique horror of war.
