Review by Kit Lazer 3

Does a wonderful job of depicting the near futility of attempting to assimilate out of addiction and homelessness. You want to enjoy each new boon and breath above the surface of the water but your past, and the guilt and trauma that comes with it, pulls you down by your ankles. It feels impossible, in that mode, to navigate a world that judges you by your actions when all you can see are your intentions. That disconnect feels like injustice and leads to just one drink and maybe just a little toot of powder to get through the day. 

Then you’re back down below, drowning again.

Review by Robert Daniels

This is really the surprise of the festival for me. Harris Dickinson directs the hell out of “Urchin.” Unlike most actors turned directors he utilizes establishing shots. He also isn’t overly reliant on closeups, instead allowing the body to fill the frame. Frank Dillane also gives an exceptional performance as a man feverishly trying to kick his drug habit. It’s not as frantic or as skittish as what’s become the cliche approach to playing an addict. Instead it’s human, charming and desperate and sorta lithe. Did I mention there’s also a lot of dancing here? Once again, Dickinson loves watching bodies in motion. He understands that great actors like Dillane build a performance not solely across their face. They build it from the spine up.

Review by The Oscar Expert

Avoids the kind of catharsis that is often expected from addiction dramas. It’s instead brutally honest. Dickinson has us rooting Mike, while feeling disturbed and frustrated by him.

Dickinson doesn’t give us too many clues as to Mike’s past, or what there is to “overcome” for him, as this would amount to a checklist of things for him to do to heal. Mike doesn’t know himself, so healing is a mystery. The best he can seem to do is escape himself entirely, only to be slammed back into his own body.
