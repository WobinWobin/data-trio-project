Sweetie

1989

Directed by Jane Campion

Synopsis

There will not be a more extraordinary film than “Sweetie” in a long long time!

The buttoned-down, superstitious Kay is attempting to lead a normal existence with her new boyfriend Louis. That’s until Sweetie, her rampaging, devil-may-care sister, returns home after an absence, exposing the rotten roots of their family and placing a strain on Kay and Louis’ relationship.

Review by Hunter ᴴᴰ

Family dysfunction in aesthetic color schemes and compositional patterns 

Kay is scared of trees. All trees, even family trees. Much like real trees she’s afraid to water and nurture any relationship in her life. She’s quiet and idiosyncratic being the opposite of her wild sister Dawn, nicknamed Sweetie, whose surprise arrival causes strange and magical family drama. 

Jane Campion brings us into the slightly off world of a seemingly normal household. Meticulously stylistic in the unusual beauty in the common rather it’s a broken porcelain horse, funky colors, or a mirror used to reflect ourselves on. It’s sort of like Tumblr threw up before Tumblr. Amazing work from Sally Bongers and Campion.

I sat with this one for a bit. The more I think about it the more heartbreaking some moments feel. A subtle yet bold visual experience of intense emotions between sisters. 

I sadly might be more of Kay than Sweetie.

Review by Edith 4

Starts about three different threads of a story and never completely tops off any. Is this a Sylvia Plath feminist appeal against the patriarchy and the despondency in marriage? Is it about unconditional love for those destroying your life? A family dealing with extreme mental illness? Who's to say. I did enjoy the very feminine approach to the cinematography, holding on small details like Kay's twisting feet or a slowly turning fan. Didn't have enough Sophia Coppola "staring out a window and pondering moments" however. This has a very specific Aussie tone, indie mumblecore awkwardness, and artsy post-Eraserhead psychosis. Should normally be something I enjoy, but the merit of its message is jumbled.

Review by MJsays

All this formally odd, intentionally lifeless suburban fairytale stuff kind of gets under my skin, regardless of the fact that I absolutely love Campion as a director, who is, in my personal opinion, one of the best filmmakers and most distinctive voices to come out of our corner of the world. But I can’t ignore the fact that all this looks and feels like Campion was still refining her craft here. Visually, it’s pretty solid, but the writing is too esoteric/stilted to absorb as anything inherently meaningful.
