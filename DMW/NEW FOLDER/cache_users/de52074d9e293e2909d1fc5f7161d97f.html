Alice-Heart

2025

Directed by Mike Macera

Synopsis

life imitates heart.

Alice-Heart is an aimless college student residing in Philadelphia. She aspires to be a famous writer but drops out of school in her last semester of senior year on a whim. Immediately cut off from her disappointed Filipino mother and dumped by her studious boyfriend, she finds that she has to pay bills on her own for the first time. Distraught, she takes solace in her neighbor Tony, a self sufficient freelance photographer. Tony encourages her to pursue her passions and hold onto her friendships. With this in mind, Alice-Heart navigates the beginnings of adulthood and combats “adult baby” allegations along the way.

Review by ZacharyBinx 3

"My mom thinks I'm an adult baby she always has to support."

A total charmfest. Alice-Heart was a bit like me when I was her age. Aimless, ambivalent, college dropout. She falls into things by luck (or pissing someone off) and when they don't work out she acts unbothered, but she can be pretty creative in getting what she wants. Luckily for us, that doesn't always work out because she's a fabulous mess. But the script feels so real and conversational. It's not flashy by any means but that's what makes it great. Also, for a fairly new cast, there's a lot of wonderful talent here. Lissa Carandang-Sweeney as the titular Alice-Heart blew me away. Certainly a vague nostalgia watch that really gets young adults. Easily will revisit as a comfort film.

Review by Billy Ray Brewton

A delightful character comedy that owes as much to Greta Gerwig as it does to Noah Baumbach, but with a decidedly Philadelphia sensibility. If you're looking for a focused narrative, this isn't for you. If you're down to just hang out with some entertaining characters for 90-minutes, you'll be in indie movie heaven. Much of the film's success is owed to the cast, universally outstanding, even smaller roles like a guy who buys a photo and then introduces our lead characters to his 'wives'. Lissa Carandang-Sweeney soars as the titular character, while Tony McCall and Adam McAlonie steal more than their fair share of scenes. So refreshing to see a relationship between a guy and a girl that doesn't fall…

Review by Aaron Bartuska

The first thing your screenwriting professors tell you in film school is “write what you know.” The second thing they tell you is “just don’t make your protagonist a struggling writer.” Mike Macera, the driving force behind this year’s Slamdance darling Alice-Heart, must not have heard that second part. His directorial debut follows Alice-Heart (Lissa Carandang-Sweeney) as she navigates nearly every brand of collegiate confusion — toxic professors, writer’s block, living up to her family name, not being able to make rent — all while balancing the men who see her as an object of their affection. It would also act as the perfect second film in a trilogy that begins with Lady Bird (2017) and ends with Frances Ha…
