A passionate telling of the story of Sada Abe, a woman whose affair with her master led to an obsessive and ultimately destructive sexual relationship.

Review by Neil Bahadur 14

This review may contain spoilers. I can handle the truth.

The key moment comes at about one hour and twenty minutes into the film, where Kichi (for the first time in the film, I think?) walks outside by himself, and we see marching soldiers and children waving Japanese nationalist flags. Kichi looks downward - maybe in shame, maybe in protest, maybe both. But this moment recontextualizes everything that has come before and contextualizes everything that will come: this hidden world that Sada and Kichi create - the 'realm of the senses' so to speak, is one made not as an extension of culture, but as a kind of suspension from it. Not just sex, but love becomes a reaction against the world.
