2023

Directed by Matteo Garrone

Synopsis

Longing for a brighter future, two Senegalese teenagers embark on a journey from West Africa to Italy. However, between their dreams and reality lies a labyrinth of checkpoints, the Sahara Desert, and the vast waters of the Mediterranean.

Review by Robert (he/him) 18

Spineless aestheticization of migration. Italy and the EU go completely untouched. There are plenty of strong (non)fictional films out there tackling the same perspective, but that feel way more authentic, haunting and insightful. The complete opposite of Holland’s excellent Green Border, Garrone’s shallow take offers nothing new, and teaches us absolutely zero about the urge to migrate and the mechanisms behind human trafficking. Moments of magic realism miss the point. The last shot for sure feels powerful, especially due to that excellent lead performance by promising newcomer Seydou Sarr, but at the same time it’s frustratingly toothless because of the out of place adventure that went before it.

Review by André 5

Talk about The Hero's Journey! These two 16 years-old Senegalese have the dream of going to Europe earn a living and send some money back home. The film is about their journey, so I thought it would be a typical story of hardship during their trip, but I couldn't have been more wrong. What these two boys go through is unimaginable and soon the initial childish hope gives way to fear and despair in ever greater proportions. Still, it's a wonderful and moving saga.

While in some Hollywood films we see actors speaking English with an accent pretending to be Italian, Io Capitano is an Italian production mostly spoken in Wolof and this only makes the work even more compelling. Certainly a well-deserved Oscar nomination.

A coming-of-age odyssey by two Senegalese kids trying to escape poverty and find a better life for themselves. As with any odyssey, they would have to endure a lot of things, from hunger to the heat of the desert, tumultuous water, racism, and apparently some form of torture chamber (like tf was that all about?). Review by Nikhil Das 2

This film made me go through spectrum of emotions. It was extremely painful to watch at some scenes and also made me feel wholesome and happy for Seydou's journey by the end. It's a gritty, brutal take on immigrant or refugee lives, However the director Matteo Garrone didn't slimmer the hopes or optimism of Seydou and Moussa die. Both of the Senegalese actors deliver such an incredible and honest performances, Their naivety, optimism, excitement, resilience and agony through their journey of unexpected ordeals and horrors they go through felt very realistic. Especially Seydou Sarr, I can't believe that's his debut role. I shouldn't say this but it was beautifully shot film especially those desert scenes of Sahara, it was very immersive yet devastating experience for me.

The ending scene where Seydou shouts 'I am the captain!' made my day.
