Bridge of Spies

2015

Directed by Steven Spielberg

Synopsis

In the shadow of war, one man showed the world what we stood for.

During the Cold War, the Soviet Union captures U.S. pilot Francis Gary Powers after shooting down his U-2 spy plane. Sentenced to 10 years in prison, Powers' only hope is New York lawyer James Donovan, recruited by a CIA operative to negotiate his release. Donovan boards a plane to Berlin, hoping to win the young man's freedom through a prisoner exchange. If all goes well, the Russians would get Rudolf Abel, the convicted spy who Donovan defended in court.

Review by Josh Lewis

Think it was Dargis who pointed out this great quote from John Le Carré: "for how long can we defend ourselves by methods of this kind, and still remain the kind of society that is worth defending?" and though the literal 'methods' may have changed the call for humanity remains the same. It’s important that this is a series of backroom conversations between ordinary people, and that Hanks' Donovan is capable of shaping them to his goal not because he's a particularly great lawyer/negotiator but because of a basic conviction in decency. Hokey and reductive, sure, but effective all the same.

A film where Hanks goes salmon fishing. There are no bridges here... no spies... Nothing to see.

Spielberg keeps probing and probing and probing with a remarkably steady hand. It’s hard to complain about how flawlessly the film manages to remain its own kind of chessboard, driven by human impulses rather than just spectacle. As a whole, it may be somewhat simplified, but this is precisely where Spielberg’s greatest strength lies....humanism. At its core, this is a Cold War spy thriller that never really feels like one. There’s a deliberate sense of distance throughout, and Charman’s original draft, refined and sharpened by the Coens, succeeds in turning Hanks’s Donovan into a clear embodiment of what the film is ultimately trying…

Tom Hanks and Mark Rylance deliver splendid performances. Despite being a heavy dialogue driven narrative, the political tension surrounding the cat and mouse game between both countries make the drama compelling and a highly rewarding watch.
