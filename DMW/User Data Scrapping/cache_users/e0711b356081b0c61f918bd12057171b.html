Review by reibureibu 25

Uses nearly every opportunity at the start to bait you into thinking this is going to be another trope-y anime cringefest before pulling back each time and opting to tell one of the most gosh-darn sweet stories where still-growing characters and their fluctuating relationships actually have room for messy explorations of their love (of self, of others, of life) that's graced with a surprising maturity rare for its ilk. Doesn't necessarily seek to explode past the boundaries of its canvas like past Yuasas, but it doesn't need to; the animation serves to bolster the development instead of overtake it, a wise decision that results in creative perspectives of the world around us as if we were seeing it anew along…

Review by Jake 5

“Let’s spend all of our Christmases together,”

So Masaaki Yuasa just up and decided to make a Makoto Shinkai movie, like an anime fusion of The Shape Of Water, Lost in Translation, and Anohana. As unbelievably charming as it is emotionally devastating, filled with detail upon incredible detail, and while it’s restrained by Yuasa’s standards, it’s also just stupidly gorgeous to look at. Couple in some great characters, deftly handled interwoven narrative arcs about grief, memory, empathy, and the unspoken connection we have with one another through shared acts of kindness, and we have what is, so far, my favorite movie of this year.... yeah it didn’t have a lot of competition but sue me, beautiful sappy anime melodrama is my bread and butter. I also cried in front of friends so that was nice.
