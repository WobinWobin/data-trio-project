Cold Sweat

1970

Directed by Terence Young

Synopsis

Bronson and Mason play a high stakes game of cat and mouse . . . and only one man can walk away the winner

During the Korean War, Joe Moran, is convicted for striking a colonel. Imprisoned in Germany he encounters his former company commander Captain Ross, jailed for black marketeering. Together with Ross' cohorts Joe agrees to escape with them, but things go wrong and a policeman is killed. An appalled Joe escapes by himself, abandoning the others, who are recaptured. Years later Joe has a boat rental business where he is found by Ross. Now a wanted drug smuggler he wants the use of Joe's boats and to ensure his cooperation, they kidnap his wife and daughter.

Review by HKFanatic

At first you think this is going to be a proto-"John Wick," with Charles Bronson as a boat captain living a quiet life with his family off the coast of France until his criminal past comes back to haunt him...but it's like 1970 didn't quite have the vocabulary for this kind of action film yet, so "Cold Sweat" relies on traditional Hitchcockian suspense, and the whole affair feels far too staged and talky, particularly coming from the director of "From Russia with Love"! Case in point: Bronson never once uses a shotgun like he's holding on the poster art.

There's admittedly some novelty to seeing two actors of the caliber of Liv Ullmann and James Mason wind up in a Charles Bronson vehicle, but they're not well served by this material. My pulse quickened during a late film car chase, but that's about it. (And there's a similar car chase in 71's "The Last Run," a much better movie).

Review by Saint_Hooligan

Winter trash 2026

7. 01/07 - Watch a movie with the word "cold" in the title.

Charles Bronson spends most of this movie looking mildly annoyed while cruising around the French Riviera, which, honestly, is already a solid selling point. He plays a former criminal who’s tried to go straight, only to have his past come roaring back when his old crew of scummy traffickers kidnaps his wife and daughter and forces him into one last job. This isn’t peak Bronson vengeance mode, but it’s got just enough grit to keep things interesting. The pacing can feel sluggish and the villains’ accents are all over the map. Still, there’s a genuinely solid car chase, some sweaty Euro-thriller atmosphere, and Bronson’s trademark stone-faced intensity doing a lot of heavy lifting. It’s not a classic, but it’s perfectly watchable. Come for the brooding Bronson and scenic locations, stay because you’re already halfway through and it’s honestly not that bad.

Review by Jacob Knight

The first in a three film run Bronson would make with OG Bond architect Terence Young while crafting the next stage of his career in France. Adapted from a Richard Matheson story, it’s kinda like his HISTORY OF VIOLENCE: a charter boat Captain in a small fishing village finds his wife (Liv Ullman) and twelve-year-old stepdaughter kidnapped by a gang of Charlie’s unsavory ex-comrades (led by James Mason!) after they recognize his picture in the local paper. Stripped down to pulp essentials and beautifully photographed, it climaxes in a breakneck car chase through the rocky, winding hills of the Alpes-Maritimes. One of the best of Bronson’s Euro thrillers.

[Source: Kino Lorber Blu-ray]
