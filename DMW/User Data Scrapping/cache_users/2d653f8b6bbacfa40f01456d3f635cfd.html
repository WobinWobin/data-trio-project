Review by Travis Lytle

Derided as camp, ignored as contemporaneously inconsequential, and underappreciated in general, John Guillermin's "King Kong" is worlds better than its reputation suggests. A mid-1970s remake of the 1933 classic, the adventure film is earnest, emotionally vibrant, and respectful of its cinematic roots. From its mysterious opening to its heart-wrenching climax, "King Kong" is a completely engaging and smartly assembled genre outing; and it is an excellent piece of 1970s popcorn cinema.

Guillermin's version of "King Kong" trades the original film's crew of filmmakers for oil prospectors heading into uncharted regions of the ocean in search of petroleum-based riches.
