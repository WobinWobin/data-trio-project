The Earrings of Madame de...

1953

Madame de…

Directed by Max Ophüls

Synopsis

It was her vanity that destroyed her.

In late 19th century France, the Countess Louise, wife of a wealthy general, sells the earrings her husband gave her on their wedding day to pay off her secret debts, then claims to have lost them. Her husband quickly learns of the deceit, which is the beginning of many tragic misunderstandings, all involving the earrings, the general, the countess, and her new lover, the Italian Baron Donati.

Review by Ethan Colburn

This movie feels like a dream.

It is so beautifully and precisely shot, weaving an impossible tale about a woman and her earrings' journey across multiple transactions and suitors. The movie has several ongoing gags which it never drops, including the fact that you never know "Madame de..."'s name. The cinematography is stunning, including some highly technical shots that are executed with an incredible level of ease.

Tonally, it balances being funny, sad, and romantic perfectly. I hope to dive more into Max Ophüls.

Review by Paul Elliott 2

Max Ophüls' adaptation of Louise de Vilmorin's well-known 1951 French novel features virtues seldom in attendance in many comparably themed films, and what could have been a rather conventional period romance is pivoted into an ornate mystery through his wonderful skills as a filmmaker. Similarly to the excellent Letter From an Unknown Woman, the filmmaker concentrates on how the overwhelming distress of a female protagonist goes unseen by her male companions.

There's plenty of visually delightful cinematography with sweeping views and characters blowing in and out of the frame, it's a veritable banquet for the eyes, and the earrings themselves are treated unsurprisingly as a character in their own right; they travel internationally and are interweaved together with many of the personalities of the film. The Earrings of Madame de... is a magnificent movie crowded with protagonists who have a truly warped sense of judgement.
