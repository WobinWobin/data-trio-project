Where the last APES was almost complete rehash and ripoff of the first, this one is a near-total inversion of the original; instead of three astronauts crash-landing on a strange planet, three Apes land on ours. It continues the storyline of the previous two movies (an impressive feat in and of itself given how BENEATH THE PLANET OF THE APES ended) but ESCAPE FROM THE PLANET OF THE APES is otherwise a wholly unique animal; it's not an action-adventure movie, it's a fish-out-of-water comedy. At least until, in typical APES fashion, things take a turn for the dark and disturbing.

Review by Sethsreviews 2

An total inverse of the original film, while also easily being one of the most character-driven films in the franchise. The first half being welcoming and absurd, with the apes on Earth learning other ways—a truly out-of-place scenario that strengthens the viewer's bond with Cornelius and Zira and their relationship. However, the second half swiftly transforms into a dark, dangerous escapade with infinite political concerns and moral quandaries, akin to the first two films, but with a completely new setting and approach. Given that it's virtually two films in one, the balance between sincere satire and true thriller is excellent, concluding in one of the franchise's most emotional climaxes.

Review by George Carmi 5

I don't find myself connecting with these films the way others seem to. 1968s film felt game-changing and it was really cool experiencing it for the first time, but these subsequent sequels have felt lazy, repetitive, and almost spoof-ey. Franchises have their familiar beats, but its a bit too much with these films. It was fun changing the POV on us but that intrigue lasted about 15 minutes before we slowly drag around a bland narrative. This movie feels very elementary, kind of like something you'd show a class of 6th graders in order to teach them morals and values at the most basic level.
