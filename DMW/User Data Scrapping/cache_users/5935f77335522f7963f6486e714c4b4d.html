Dancer in the Dark

2000

Directed by Lars von Trier

Synopsis

In a world of shadows, she found the light of life.

Selma, a Czech immigrant on the verge of blindness, struggles to make ends meet for herself and her son, who has inherited the same genetic disorder and will suffer the same fate without an expensive operation. When life gets too difficult, Selma learns to cope through her love of musicals, dreaming up little numbers to the rhythmic beats of her surroundings.

Review by Jacob 21

I understand why there are movies about war. I see purpose in making movies that center on tragedy and misery to make greater thematic declarations about humanity. Several of my favorite films have challenging subject matter or include devastating sequences, but every miserable film is not inherently profound. Sometimes movies are overly manipulative, displaying horrific images and sounds to force a reaction that is ultimately meaningless.

The admittedly-bold musical format presents the story of Selma, an immigrant who comes to the United States for a better future and ends up suffering at every possible turn. The lack of emotional grounding makes this a purely unpleasant experience, rather than a moving one. The shaky, documentary-esque aesthetic only further distorts the emotion…
