All About My Mother

1999

Todo sobre mi madre

Directed by Pedro Almodóvar

Synopsis

Part of every woman is a mother/actress/saint/sinner. And part of every man is a woman.

Following the tragic death of her teenage son, Manuela travels from Madrid to Barcelona in an attempt to contact the long-estranged father the boy never knew. She reunites with an old friend, an outspoken transgender sex worker, and befriends a troubled actress and a pregnant, HIV-positive nun.

Review by Josh Larsen 11

One of the delightful pleasures of All About My Mother is the way these chaotic characters fall so easily into each other’s upended lives, so that those in need of care become indistinguishable from the caregivers. Rosa tries to find Manuela a job, only to have Manuela become her unofficial doula. Huma grants Manuela a favor, only to be greatly comforted by her presence. Almodovar’s trademark empathy is in full flower here, as All About My Mother suggests that the best way to care for someone is simply to be there.

Review by Ana V 2

Almodovar is possibly one of the few male directors who actually knows how to pay homage to women.

As it’s made clear with the final scene, this film is a love letter to all the women in Pedro’s life, to the women who nurtured him, who made an impression on him, who inspired him.

You can tell he understands women by how well every single one of these characters is written. They portray the best and the worst sides of womanhood, and it’s one of the most realistic stories I’ve seen on screen even with how outlandish it is at the same time.

And the movie is definitely carried by the extraordinary actresses who injected life and spirit into their respective roles. What a masterpiece.
