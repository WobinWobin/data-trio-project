Every Which Way but Loose

1978

Directed by James Fargo

Synopsis

Clint Eastwood will turn you Every Which Way But Loose

Philo Beddoe is your regular, easygoing, truck-driving guy. He's also the best bar-room brawler west of the Rockies. And he lives with a 165-pound orangutan named Clyde. Like other guys, Philo finally falls in love - with a flighty singer who leads him on a screwball chase across the American Southwest. Nothing's in the way except a motorcycle gang, some cops, and legendary brawler Tank Murdock.

Review by 📀 Cammmalot 📀 7

”Clyde, this has gotta stop.
People are gonna talk, ya know?”

This is a incredibly poorly directed, and an unbelievably stupid movie…but it’s so fun-loving that you just want to forgive it. Nearly everything about the plot doesn’t work, but whenever the movie forgets about the plot it becomes truly enjoyable. There’s a fantastic shot of Clint & Clyde just driving in the truck and you can totally tell how much fun Eastwood is having. I could have watched that for hours.

Normally when I hear the words period piece I think of butlers, horses and fancy costumes, but I’d argue that this movie is so much of the 70’s that nowadays it could rightfully be called a period piece.

BONUS POINTS for the casting. It was such a joy to see favorites like Hank Worden with his unmistakable line readings and Ruth Gordon hamming it up as Ma.

Review by Justin Decloux

The title is ironic, because a looser picture could not exist.

A 48-year old Clint is the best fighter in the world, who lives with his mom, and owns an orangutan. You'd think the smiling simian would be the films focus, but you would be incorrect. Clyde's just along for the jaunty ride as Eastwood obsessively stalks a 24-year old Sondra Locke cross country. He gets in a lot of sloppy fisticuffs, goes and sees strippers with his furry pal, and listens to some good country music. Every face that wanders on screen is wrinkly and leathered.

You could tune in at any point and think you're 30 minutes in.
