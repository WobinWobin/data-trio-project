Popeye

1980

Directed by Robert Altman

Synopsis

Haves a happy holiday with me an' Olive!

Popeye is a super-strong, spinach-scarfing sailor man who's searching for his father. During a storm that wrecks his ship, Popeye washes ashore and winds up rooming at the Oyl household, where he meets Olive. Before he can win her heart, he must first contend with Olive's fiancé, Bluto.

Review by Will Sloan 4

The closest Hollywood has ever come to Playtime. Confounding on the first 10 viewings, but fully becomes a masterpiece around viewing #50, by which time every single citizen of Sweethaven will feel like your friend. Shelley Duvall should have gone home with Mr. Oscar. The "Everything is Food" number is as good as cinema gets. The production ran out of money and so the last stretch admittedly suffers, but even when this movie isn't working it's still fun to spend time in.

The new Blu-Ray is stunning, showcasing Giuseppe Rotunno's cinematography like I've never seen before. Every shot is so full of detail that it actually hurts to watch a little.

Review by Branson Reese 2

D.A.R.E. did an assembly at my elementary school in the 90s to warn us about this movie. "Sure, it's a barrel of laughs to watch Robin Williams eat spinach and riff with a baby. But this movie is a gateway drug to much harder Altmans" they warned. "Before you know it, you'll be hooked on his overlapping dialogue and defiant spirit. You'll be desperate to get your fix from McCabe & Ms. Miller or California Split. And it won't stop there." They dimmed the lights in our gymnasium. "Soon you'll be Beyond Therapy." You could hear a pin drop.

That night I went with my family to Blockbuster and rented Popeye. As with most War on Drugs era propaganda, I hadn't been discouraged at all. I had been introduced.

Review by Dave Jackson 4

What in fuck is this movie? Popeye is up there with the strangest studio movies ever made. A rambling, aimless nightmare that kind of beautifully captures the spirit and madcappery of a Fleischer animation while also relentlessly alienating the viewer. Characters repeat lines over and over. Most dialogue is muttered, meaningless, or talked over. Every action is deliberate, wobbly, and utterly bizarre. With his disgusting prosthetic arms, Robin Williams is a surprisingly excellent Popeye. Paul L. Smith is a walking horrorshow as Bluto. But Shelley Duvall as Olive Oyl blows everyone out of the water. She not only looks exactly like the original character, but every movement she makes gives her the appearance of a flesh and blood cartoon. The music by Harry Nilsson, much like the rest of the film, is as jarring as it is oddly wonderful. As completely unwatchable as Popeye is, I think I love it?
