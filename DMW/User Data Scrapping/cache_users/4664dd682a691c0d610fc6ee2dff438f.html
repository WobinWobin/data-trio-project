Sure, she is the victim of exploitation in the film industry, but she gives herself willingly, seeing no other choice.

Review by Christina

“you’ve already snatched away what i wanted.” 

the blinding lights of the entertainment industry beam down on nina wu, revealing the tattered relationship between our titular character’s internal and external identities, through the picture’s own misty narrative. writer and lead, wu ke-xi delivers a flawless performance—eerily examining the disorienting psyche of obsessive artistry and the pervading exploitation of its predators. 

up and coming actress, nina wu reluctantly accepts a role that requires nudity, believing it’ll be her big break. reminiscent of perfect blue and the black swan, and hot on the heels of the #me too movement, the film applies the industry’s pernicious pattern of sexual misconduct and power abuse, to an anxiety-inducing, taiwanese experience of crippling vulnerability descending to a personal abyss. stunningly shot and tragically felt—a tad repetitive and gimmicky treading into its third act but an overall blazing effort portraying the sludge women endure in show biz.

Review by Paul Elliott

Written by leading actress Wu Ke-xi, Nina Wu delivers a timely exploration of the incremental erosion of one's own collection of beliefs in the pursuit of a dream in a savagely patriarchal and fiercely conservative culture. Set within the Taiwanese capital Taipei, the eponymous protagonist seizes the opportunity to leave her minor theatre company in the countryside when given a chance to further her career as an actress with a prominent film role in the city. 

She comes to realise that a massive emotional expense accompanies the road to stardom, and she becomes put through a succession of humiliation and violence as she struggles to make her way through the oppressive gender and sexual politics of her situation. It frequently has overtones…

Review by Paul Thomas

Nina Wu is a #metoo movie, and it makes no mistake of its intentions, yet its highly stylized and thematically large. It's mysterious and creepy, capturing what appears to be the psychological damage of abuse that imprisons you with your own trauma.

It doesn't necessarily focus on the intricacies and nuances of abuse, unlike what the great film The Assistant captured. But in my mind, it's not a knock against Nina Wu, but rather another look at the more acute and blunt ramifications of abuse. Both views can and should exist.

It's a film that feels like it goes from The Shining to Lynch to Donnie Darko. Is this all an exaggerated view? You'd think, and would hope, yet every insane story that'd come out during metoo of shit the Harvey Weinsteins and other elite creeps would do proves that unfortunately this is no exaggeration at all.

Review by ₳ⱠØⱠ 3

No emotions involved, pose for the camera

Heartbreaking & complex. The watching experience felt like navigating a maze and you're wandering around lost for the majority of it and the last 10 minutes or so finally delivers clarity. The trajectory of the experience parallels Nina's journey through her own trauma.

On top of that it was dripping with style and Wu Ke-Xi's performance as Nina had so much depth – even more so because she co-wrote it. This definitely grew on me and the last third changed my viewing experience completely. Not sure if the romance in the middle felt relevant or cohesive but maybe it's just because the tone was so unexpectedly different than the rest of the film.

It's a female led layered psychological drama/thriller with jarring performances, a little bit of gay shit, and a revelatory ending so yes it's getting an extra half star for being thoroughly My Shit™
