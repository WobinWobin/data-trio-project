Review by KYK 1

I love when a not-much-happens movie is done very well, and this is a great example of that. Not much happening is also the bane of Lizzy's (Michelle Williams) existence — she's a ceramic sculptor stifled by an art-adjacent yet administrative job and the lack of hot water in her house (her landlady Jo is also an artist with a show coming up, so she's too busy to address that). Jo, by the way, is played by Hong Chau, who is scary-good at wielding White Woman Next Door vibes.

Review by Filipe Furtado 2

Michelle Williams reacting to things is a great film genre. We don't get many movies about creative labor and compromises behind it (as opposite to movies about creation as an idea) so this is refreshing concrete and developed with the eye and care one would expect from Reichardt. The performances in particular have grounded physical effect that is very pleasing. Some of the family scenes are less successful and the ending is a little easier as a tie thing together idea than ideal, but this feels like very minor misgivings. For a movie with very little events, Reichardt does so much towards creating tension and anticipation.

Review by Thomas Flight 3

Reichardt’s latest is masterfully understated and deceptively layered. You’re going to have to dig a little but it’s there, in all it’s quirky Portlandia glory. “You have to listen to what isn’t being said.” 

Vibing to a anti-drama about struggling artists, where the stakes on the surface are the stress levels of a pigeon and how much cheese is put out at your gallery show is not everyone’s cup of Joe, but I love this film too death and am glad Reichardt is putting stories like this on screen.
