Streets of Fire

1984

Directed by Walter Hill

Synopsis

Tonight is what it means to be young.

Raven Shaddock and his gang of merciless biker friends kidnap rock singer Ellen Aim. Ellen's former lover, soldier-for-hire Tom Cody, happens to be passing through town on a visit. In an attempt to save his star act, Ellen's manager hires Tom to rescue her. Along with a former soldier, they battle through dangerous cityscapes, determined to get Ellen back.

Review by Todd Gaines 58

There's a couple points of key points that instantly come to my mind while watching Walter Hill's Streets of Fire. 

One, I so dig the 1950's / 1980's mashup. The 50's cars, slang and street gangs; combined with 80's fashion, vibe and sound. I also like how some of the fashion and music are a hybrid of both decades. Think Blue Velvet, but without the PBR. Any guesses what year this takes place in?

Second, why don't more people rave about this Rock-N'-Roll-action-packed-love-story-rock-opera? Is it because it tanked at the box office? I'm not sure, but I would rank Streets of Fire as one of Walter Hill's best movies and quite possibly his most fun film. 

Hill writes a jamming…

Review by laird 2

"It's the shits" - Rick Moranis' character says this as an insult, but I'm saying it as praise. Streets of Fire is the shits.

I really enjoyed spending 90 minutes in that 50s by way of the 80s movie backlot universe where the dialog might as well be in cartoon bubbles and the roles haven't evolved since the days of mustache twirling villains tying the hero's girl to railroad tracks. Everything is amped up and loud, like maybe cocaine was introduced in the chemical bath when the negative was being developed. Could have ended five minutes sooner, because that last song... woof!

MVP: Tie between Willem Dafoe's lip quiver and Bill Paxton's hair.
