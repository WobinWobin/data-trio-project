Contact

1997

Directed by Robert Zemeckis

Synopsis

Take a journey to the heart of the universe.

A radio astronomer receives the first extraterrestrial radio signal ever picked up on Earth. As the world powers scramble to decipher the message and decide upon a course of action, she must make some difficult decisions between her beliefs, the truth, and reality.

Review by ChrisStuckmann

Possibly my favorite opening shot of all time.

Two of the most memorable theatrical experiences of my childhood were seeing Apollo 13 and Contact. Both PG-rated films about space exploration aimed at adults, and both tame enough to be for kids, too. Contact captured me immediately. I already had an interest in aliens thanks to The X-Files and a ton of grade school genre books, but this film’s scientific approach (thanks Carl Sagan), really put a grounded spin on it. For a few years, I truly considered going into astronomy. 

As an adult, this film is astonishing. A technician behind the camera, Zemeckis works wonders. There is not one single pedestrian shot in the movie. Everything feels alive and moving. The VFX hold up still, the wormhole sequence being a highlight. Foster is impeccable. Silvestri is magical. A highlight of the 90s.
