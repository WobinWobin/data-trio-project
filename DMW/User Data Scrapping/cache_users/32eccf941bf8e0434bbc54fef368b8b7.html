Review by Eli Hayes 7

This review may contain spoilers.

I feel like someone put me in my refrigerator for the last 1.5 hours. Seriously one of the most bewildering things I've seen all year with such an aesthetically misguided and disconcerting denouement that I'm (as expected) not sure what to make of its tremendously bizarre tone & the overall directorial approach to the actually rather solemn and somber topic examined herein. Like we're against corruption, greed and violence, so let's celebrate and nod our heads to hopeful/opmistic music while Neil walks through the physical remnants of a mass genocide?? This might be the very strangest work he's crafted yet. Mr. Breen, I would love to sit down and have a conversation with you about ourselves,…

Review by Ziglet_mir 13

Red lights and Immigrants and Tigers... oh my!

Neil Breen is amazingly consistent in his vision and technical prowess. It’s hard to argue his films are beyond the “it’s so bad it’s good” niche, but I actually think they are. The overall vision is what convinces me otherwise. Breen’s usual thematic tropes of taking down “The Man” and all that encompass that are steadfastly present; politicians, CEOs, reporters, journalists, immigrants and immigration enforcers, drug dealers, activists, thugs and estranged family members. No one gets a pass from Breen unless they are pure. Pure of what though, is the question. As some of my fellow Breeners have astutely observed, Breen may very well be the greatest force of ideological centrism ever witnessed…
