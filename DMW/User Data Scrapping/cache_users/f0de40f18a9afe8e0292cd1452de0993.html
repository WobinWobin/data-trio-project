Review by Sean Fennessey 14

I spent a couple hours with Gus Van Sant when Don't Worry He Won’t Get Far on Foot came out seven years ago. He was utterly bemused by podcasting and even the level of modest inquiry in the conversation. He arrived alone. It was a quiet, simple discussion. He certainly seemed like someone who was over it all. He was 65 and had pretty much seen it all.

His stretch of films, from Drugstore Cowboy to Good Will Hunting, followed closely by the press coverage (if not the text) of his Psycho remake contributed a great deal to my growing cinephilia as a teenager. I’ve always admired the breadth of that run and the Cheshire Cat quality his films possess,…
