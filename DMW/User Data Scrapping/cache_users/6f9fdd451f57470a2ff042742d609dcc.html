The Twentieth Century

2019

Directed by Matthew Rankin

Synopsis

Blistering shame! Unbridled passions! Pulse-quickening tribulation!

Toronto, Canada, 1899. William Lyon Mackenzie King (1874-1950) fervently believes that he is destined to become Prime Minister, but to do so he will first have to fight his personal obsessions and overcome the many obstacles he will encounter on his tortuous path to power.

Review by Justin Decloux

My favourite film of midnight madness and the best Canadian film of the year. It's got everything a good citizen of the dominion needs: Narwhal impalement, insanely intricate sets, and a deadly ice maze of death to choose the next prime minister. The trailer highlights the silliness and denseness of the visuals, but what it doesn't indicate is how compelling it all is. Prime Minister William MacKenzie King could have been played as a clueless fop, but instead, Dan Beirne gives him a doomed sadness that invests the narrative with actual stakes. He wants to be a spineless centrist, but the powers that be want him to sell out beyond even what he could imagine!

See it.

Downright hilarious and a stylistic powerhouse of originality. I think I would have liked this even more if I was more tuned in to Canadian politics, but even as its own entity, I thought this was great. Mackenzie King might be one of my favorite characters of the year so far, and the performance from Dan Beirne was stellar. The humor and the visual style, reminiscent of a Rankin/Bass production, but with live action characters and extremely crude and unnerving jokes, ejaculating cacti, boot fucking, baby seal clubbing and all.
