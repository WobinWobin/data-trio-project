The Country Bears

2002

Directed by Peter Hastings

Synopsis

They’re legends. Bearly.

For Beary Barrington, The Country Bears' young #1 fan, fitting in with his all-too-human family is proving im-paws-ible. When he runs away to find Country Bear Hall and his heroes, he discovers the venue that made them famous is near foreclosure. Beary hightails it over the river and through the woods to get the Bears in the Band back together for an all-out reunion concert to save Country Bear Hall.

Review by Matt Singer 3

It’s truly amazing that Disney adapted this before they got around to Pirates of the Caribbean, Haunted Mansion, or Space Mountain. And it’s truly insane that they did it this way, not by mimicking the folksy vibe of the show from Disneyland, but by basically turning it into The Blues Brothers with animatronic bears. And then it’s really galaxy brained that the songs are genuinely lovely and catchy, and not at all the parody songs and country music spoofs of the Disneyland show. Instead, these goofy, lumbering, almost believable bears open their mouths and the voices of Don Henley and Bonnie Raitt come out of them. It’s just jaw-on-the-floor bizarre — and that’s before Christopher Walken shows up to do some musical armpit farts.

I honestly have no idea how to even give this movie a star rating. It is beyond such earthly matters.

Review by Dan McCoy 6

I could write a whole, very long essay about why I found this movie so goddamn funny, and I'm pondering doing just that, but I'll keep it shorter for now.

The central, craziest decision they made when deciding to make an adaptation of the Disneyland attraction "The Country Bears," which is just a bunch of country-singing animatronic bears (which is already a crazy decision) is this -- they decide to not make a big deal out of the fact there are bears in it.

Like, in this movie -- you'll be at a normal human party, but there are bears at the buffet, but you, the human will behave as if this is not a bear in front of you.…
