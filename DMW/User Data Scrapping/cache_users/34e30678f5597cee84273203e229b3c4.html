Review by SilentDawn 4

86/100

Roy Andersson's Songs from the Second Floor is analogous to a rambling and insane homeless man on the side of the highway; his distant screams and cries of anguish may seem humorous at first because of their dry annotations and blunt audacity, but his wailing pleas soon creep into your soul. The world that Roy Andersson depicts, as hilariously strange and melancholic as it is, never feels too far off from our own. A truly bizarre, unusual, and astonishing piece of cinematic art.

Review by nick 2

Songs from the Second Floor is to conventional cinema what poem is to a material world. Director Roy Andersson knew only the most absurd and surreal form of storytelling would do his grand visions justice, and Songs from the Second Floor is exactly that nutcase messenger Andersson needs to broadcast his images of poetic philosophy.

Songs from the Second Floor consists of multiple seemingly loosely-related segments, with high level of surrealism, bleak shots and dry humor. It's a distant yet all too familiar world as we witness the crumbling of the stock market, the dying of faith, upper class performing human sacrifice on a girl to save the day, and the protagonist's own struggles with ghosts from the guilty past,…

Review by shookone 2

the Andersson that probably feels most like a kafkaesque fever dream. full blown existential dread from the cold hell of Scandinavia. pain and suffering is individual and detaches you from the rest of society, most palpable in the moment of emotional unrest and agitation. men in grey suits and voluptuous & saggy women have nothing to say to each other anymore. the church is a joke. and then there's still the repressed memories of fascism, holocaust and death.

Review by Dirk Diggler

"it's not easy being human"

The bare, grey sets, the cast and its sad characters with their extremely pale faces, the static camera, the wonderful dialogue, the whole aesthetic and mis-en-scene comes together to create the most miserable, depressing world i've ever seen depicted, but it is an oddly striking observation and look into our modern civilization and on the strange and pitiful behavior of human beings. The composition of each set is so clever as it really conveys the tedious and depressing existence of these characters, and the balance between its dry, deadpan humor and bleak themes works tremendously well. Songs from the Second Floor is a wonderful film on many levels with many great scenes throughout, a profound, funny and dark reflection on the human condition.
