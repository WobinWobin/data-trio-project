Hi, Mom!

1970

Directed by Brian De Palma

Synopsis

God bless our humble upper-middle-class high-rise co-op and keep it free from smut peddlers, militants, urban guerrillas and Greenwich Village liberals.

Vietnam vet Jon Rubin returns to New York and rents a rundown flat in Greenwich Village. It is in this flat that he begins to film, 'Peeping Tom' style, the people in the apartment across the street. His obsession with making films leads him to fall in with a radical 'Black Power' group, which in turn leads him to carry out a bizarre act of urban terrorism.

Review by Holli 6

horny rear window. american psycho if patrick bateman was a filmmaker. a taxi driver prequel snl skit. an r-rated 60s sitcom. incel agnès varda film.

these are all comparisons that popped into my head while watching hi, mom! but i feel like none of them do justice to just how strangely wonderful this film. de palma tackles an era of rising pornography, racism/prejudice coming to a boiling point, changing technology, innovation in filmmaking, and the timelessness of men being shit. tackling so many themes in one movie shouldn't work as well as it does, but somehow he pulled it off. at least for me, anyway. this definitely won’t work for everyone—not even close—but, fuck, i loved it. i don’t even know if it’s “good”, but, oh, it’s so good.

Review by Trevor

Hi, Mom! is Brian De Palma's "Jean-Luc Godard makes a porno." Because, after all, if Godard did make porn, he would undoubtedly seek to analyze desire, so to speak, and break it down into its constituent parts, forming links with the rest of the world. This process of linking is central to Hi, Mom!, which intercuts between story lines that are initially separate, one (focusing on the film's protagonist, Robert De Niro's Jon Rubin, who begins as an amateur pornographer) obviously more dominant (and not arbitrarily so, but very critically dominant) than the other (focusing on black radical group staging a strange, insurrectionary performance art piece entitled "Be Black, Baby"), but that converge in very meaningful ways.
