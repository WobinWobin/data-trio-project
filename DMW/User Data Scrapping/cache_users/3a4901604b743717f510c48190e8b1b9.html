Blitz

2024

Directed by Steve McQueen

Synopsis

A boy's journey. A mother's love. Miles of burning city between them.

In World War II London, nine-year-old George is evacuated to the countryside by his mother, Rita, to escape the bombings. Defiant and determined to return to his family, George embarks on an epic, perilous journey back home as Rita searches for him.

Review by davidehrlich

It’s strange to discover that “Blitz” is the most anonymous movie that Steve McQueen has made thus far, as this pseudo-Dickensian epic — the story of a half-Grenadian boy’s quest to reunite with his guilt-ridden single mother (Saoirse Ronan) after she evacuates him out of London in the fall of 1940 — would appear to be an ideal showcase for his singular vision as a filmmaker.

Drawn towards subjects that allow him to interrogate and expand upon historical notions of resilience, the “Hunger” director has frequently returned to portraits of life during wartime over the course of his career as both a visual artist and commercial auteur. Review by Tyler Whitmore 5

Harris Dickinson is the most random casting decision ever for this film. Dude is just there.

Man, I really did not care for this. This does not feel like the same director who made 12 Years A Slave. Saoirse Ronan was innocent, but this felt deeply thin, disjointed, and uninspired.

I know Apple is desperate for some more Oscar love after failing to bring anything home with Killers of the Flower Moon, but this oozes Oscar bait in all the wrong kinds of ways and is much more akin to Empire of Light than anything truly worthy of winning awards.

The score is there but the tension never feels earned, the performances are all great but the emotion never lands. Blitz is just missing something, or maybe it is just something that felt all too familiar.

If this works as Oscar bait, though, I hope it at least baits Saoirse Ronan some more love.
