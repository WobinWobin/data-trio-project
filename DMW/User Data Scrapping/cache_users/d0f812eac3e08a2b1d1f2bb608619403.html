Directed by Levan Akin

Synopsis

It seems Istanbul is a place where people come to disappear.

Lia, a retired teacher from Georgia, learns from her young neighbor, Achi, that her long-lost transgender niece, Tekla, has crossed the border into Turkey. In search of Tekla, Lia travels to Istanbul with the unpredictable Achi, where they explore the hidden depths of the city.

Istanbul is described as the place where people go to disappear, and after watching this film I kinda see the appeal of that. It's loud, busy, crammed together and has its own dark corners, but the film presents Istanbul as being surprisingly welcoming to people of all walks of live. It's a multicultural place with a wide variety of communities and makeshift families. It's a city with millions of stories.

Review by Darren Carver-Balsiger 2

Crossing is an empathetic and compassionate movie that avoids patronising the audience. The characters feel very real, living with their regrets and flaws, which the film slowly reveals. The many threads of the story collide and drift away to form a loose structure that keeps each moment unpredictable. This is melodramatic social realism for the 21st century, examining economic hardship and social injustice within an international world. Building upon And Then We Danced, Levan Akin finds queer pain within Georgia and places it within a story that celebrates small acts of acceptance in a misunderstanding society. Crossing is beautifully made and quietly wonderful.
