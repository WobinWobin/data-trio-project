Bobi Wine: The People's President

2022

Directed by Christopher Sharp, Moses Bwayo

Synopsis

The man, the music, the movement.

Uganda has one the youngest populations in the world and one of its most flagrantly anti-democratic governments. These are ingredients for revolution, and Bobi Wine and his wife Barbie Kyagulanyi are stirring the pot. When the charismatic Bobi, a musician and member of parliament, announces his campaign for president, Uganda’s youth are ecstatic, filling parks and streets for every speech, and singing Bobi’s anthems of peace and freedom. But then comes the crackdown, orchestrated by Yoweri Museveni, a brutal dictator who has ruled Uganda for 36 years. Bobi and his crew survive arrests, beatings, torture, riots and raids.

Review by john

The movie serves as a really good primer on what the political landscape of Uganda looks like. Still, it never goes into too many details or lingers in any moments to make them feel impactful (which I'm sure they were as they were happening.) This throws the pacing out of whack, making a watch more tedious and keeping interest hard.

I'd still recommend this to anyone interested in world politics and human rights issues. It's never a bad idea to learn something. And, it's honestly jaw-dropping that the police let any of this be filmed, given how intense they are.

Review by Eddie @ The Cottage

I'm not presenting myself as a victim because I'm nobody's victim. I'm a survivor, and I would rather represent resilience.

Bobi Wine, a famous local musician, used his influence to free Uganda from decades-long dictatorship and corruption through the only way he knows how: Music and love.

The documentary entails not just his struggles, but also the people of Uganda, specifically those who lives in rural or from lower-class families who are suffering from lack of education, electricity and any other basic daily life necessities due to the incompetence of their elect democratic government who had neglected the people and abused their powers. The brutality and suffering these people had to endure till this day is just plain heart-breaking to watch.

If anything, I do wish it will win the Oscar just so that in a sense, it will bring more awareness and sympathy to this conundrum.
