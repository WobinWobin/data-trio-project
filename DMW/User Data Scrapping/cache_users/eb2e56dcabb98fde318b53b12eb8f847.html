Dahomey

2024

Directed by Mati Diop

Synopsis

Thousands of royal artifacts of Dahomey, a West African kingdom, were taken by French colonists in the 19th century for collection and display in Paris. Centuries later, a fraction returned to their home in modern-day Benin. This dramatized documentary follows the journey of 26 of the treasures as told by cultural art historians, embattled university students, and one of the repatriated statues himself.

Review by nick

Arthouse documentary at its finest. Lupita Nyong'o's jury made the right call awarding this the Golden Bear at Berlinale this year.

Mati Diop delves deeper into her African roots, exploring the return of looted artifacts from France to Benin. Her camera morphs seamlessly between an omniscient observer and the perspective of the artifacts themselves, weaving a narrative about identity, history, and the uncertain future of a post-colonial Africa grappling with its sense of self.

Rather than relying on traditional methods—footnotes, dialogues, or a structured narrative—*Dahomey* draws strength from its atmospheric and hypnotic visuals, immersing viewers through its aesthetic language.
