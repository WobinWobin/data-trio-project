Review by Will Sloan

This review may contain spoilers. I can handle the truth.

A strange movie that had me pinging between “What is he doing?” “Oh, is this actually great??” and finally, “Is that all?” Pitched as “A modern-day reimagining of Akira Kurosawa's High and Low,” this splices the Kurosawa film’s genes with The King of Comedy. Denzel Washington plays a legendary but past-his-prime record producer, and the kidnapper turns out to be A$AP Rocky as Yung Felon, a Soundcloud rapper who idolizes Washington as Pupkin did Langford, and sees a similar route to fame. I found this a disappointment overall, but I enjoyed watching it because Lee is a madman who is incapable of making a normal movie, and it’s always fun to sift through the mess and sort through the ideas…

Review by Brandon Streussnig 4

First 40 mins feel like Lee trolling from behind the camera. An older man baffled by the ways in which the world change by the day to the point where you’re like “is he ok??” only for him to gleefully pull the rug and run the score up by 30. He was always ahead.

A wild movie full of some of his best images and ideas. A genuine magic trick that assesses modern living and, by extension, the vapid and hollow filmmaking that represents it, by being it, lulling you into a false sense of smug “oh he’s lost it” that by the time it’s all falling into place, that stupid smile had been on my face for an hour. 

Not nearly enough Ice Spice but A$AP is tremendous.
